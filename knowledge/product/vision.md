# Siddhi vision

Siddhi (सिद्धि), “From intention to accomplishment,” is a portfolio intelligence
and strategic planning system. Its purpose is to help an owner decide where to
invest attention and resources, understand the reasons for those decisions,
and see whether work is moving toward meaningful outcomes.

Portfolio intelligence is more than a consolidated project status screen.
Repositories, documents, completed tasks, and recent commits describe activity;
they do not necessarily demonstrate a usable product, publication, validated
hypothesis, or other intended result. Siddhi connects those observations to
explicit goals while preserving what remains unknown.

# Why the portfolio view matters

Information is fragmented across project inventories, engineering artifacts,
knowledge, revision history, and verification systems. Gathering it repeatedly
is expensive, and decisions made from isolated project views can miss shared
dependencies, neglected goals, or investments that no longer serve an outcome.

Internal frameworks, libraries, and developer tools can enable several products.
They can also absorb effort indefinitely without delivering an external result.
Neither commercial work nor internal engineering automatically deserves priority:
their value depends on goals, opportunities, constraints, costs, and downstream
benefits. The same reasoning applies to creative work and research.

# Intended outcomes

- Maintain a consolidated, source-aware view of projects, goals, initiatives,
  evidence, plans, and decisions without duplicating source authority.
- Identify what can be reused, what is missing, what is uncertain, and what
  deserves investigation before more work is commissioned.
- Compare continuing, building, reusing, deferring, investigating, or stopping
  an investment with explicit rationale rather than activity counts alone.
- Recognize shared capabilities and dependencies without requiring every
  internal initiative to justify itself against one commercial product.
- Preserve assumptions, reviews, and earlier plan versions so changed
  priorities can be explained when evidence or circumstances change.
- Reduce repetitive status gathering without mandatory work-hour logging.

These are intended benefits, not claims that automated analysis can determine
every outcome or that a working implementation already provides them.

# Different work, different success

The portfolio can include commercial applications, games, books, educational
material, film, music, internal platforms, experiments, and research. A small
publication and a large software product need not share lifecycle stages,
release expectations, commercial metrics, or verification criteria.

Goals can exist before repositories. Initiatives can support several goals.
Success is evaluated against each goal's declared purpose and criteria; revenue,
audience, and other commercial measurements are optional evidence, not invented
defaults. See [portfolio domain](../concepts/portfolio-domain.md).

# Three complementary capabilities

**Portfolio intelligence** consolidates attributable observations and their
coverage limitations. **Deterministic analysis** applies explicit comparisons
and produces reproducible findings. **Strategic reasoning** uses selected
evidence for deeper external synthesis when judgment is valuable.

Each has a different authority. An observation is not an assessment, a suggested
relationship is not a confirmed match, and a recommendation is not an approved
decision. The owner remains responsible for consequential choices. See
[evidence semantics](../concepts/evidence-semantics.md) and
[human-controlled decisions](../decisions/human-controlled-decisions.md).

# Boundaries and principles

Siddhi is not a replacement for project inventory, engineering knowledge,
development-artifact authoring, source control, or verification tools. Source
systems retain authority over their records; Siddhi owns its portfolio planning
and analysis context. Knowledge can exist without active delivery intent and
delivery intent can exist before adequate knowledge is documented.

Siddhi is not a generic task platform, employee-monitoring system, mandatory
timesheet, universal maturity framework, or autonomous portfolio authority.
Ordinary reporting does not need an AI provider. Routine source reading does
not grant permission to write or publish anything.

Siddhi is itself an internal investment. Its scope should remain proportionate
to demonstrated portfolio problems rather than becoming an open-ended tooling
programme. Each increment should justify the overhead it introduces.

# Direction and uncertainty

Outcome orientation, independent knowledge, deterministic routine processing,
human control, read-oriented source access, and avoidance of mandatory time
logging are established product-direction principles. This concept remains an
unverified draft; it records no human approval event.

The proposed initial backend uses Python, FastAPI, and SQLite, with a separate
desktop interface later. Those are implementation proposals, not the definition
of the vision. The core model remains independent of presentation.

Useful indicators of progress include goals represented before projects exist,
explainable recommendations, less manual reconciliation, preserved decision
rationale, and a clear distinction between documented progress and demonstrated
outcomes. Quantitative success targets, initial source coverage, the smallest
useful portfolio model, external-analysis workflows, and local recovery needs
remain open. See [scope and roadmap](scope-and-roadmap.md).
